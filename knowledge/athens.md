# Athens

## Overview

Athens is the capital of Greece and one of the world's oldest cities, with a recorded history spanning over 3,400 years. It is widely regarded as the birthplace of democracy, Western philosophy, and theater. The modern city has a population of around 3.1 million in the metropolitan area and serves as Greece's political, economic, and cultural center. Athens combines ancient monuments — most notably the Acropolis — with lively contemporary neighborhoods, a strong café culture, and access to the Athens Riviera coastline and nearby islands via the port of Piraeus.

## Top Attractions

- **Acropolis**: The hilltop citadel containing the Parthenon, Erechtheion, and Temple of Athena Nike. Visit early morning or late afternoon to avoid heat and crowds; timed entry applies in peak season.
- **Acropolis Museum**: A modern museum at the foot of the hill displaying original sculptures and artifacts from the site.
- **Ancient Agora**: The civic center of classical Athens, including the well-preserved Temple of Hephaestus.
- **National Archaeological Museum**: One of the world's most important collections of ancient Greek artifacts.
- **Plaka and Anafiotika**: Historic neighborhoods beneath the Acropolis with neoclassical houses and island-style architecture.
- **Temple of Olympian Zeus and Panathenaic Stadium**: Colossal temple ruins and the marble stadium that hosted the first modern Olympic Games in 1896.
- **Monastiraki**: A lively square and flea-market district.
- **Lycabettus Hill**: The city's highest point, offering panoramic views; accessible by funicular or footpath.

## Local Cuisine

- **Souvlaki and gyros**: Grilled meat in pita, the standard inexpensive meal (typically 3–5 EUR).
- **Mezedes**: Small shared plates such as tzatziki, dolmades, saganaki, and grilled octopus.
- **Moussaka and pastitsio**: Baked casseroles of eggplant or pasta with meat and béchamel sauce.
- **Greek salad (horiatiki)**: Tomato, cucumber, olives, and feta.
- **Coffee culture**: Freddo espresso and freddo cappuccino are ubiquitous; cafés function as social hubs.
- **Desserts**: Baklava, loukoumades (honey doughnuts), and Greek yogurt with honey.

Tavernas in neighborhoods such as Psyrri, Koukaki, and Pangrati generally offer better value than those on Plaka's main tourist streets.

## Transportation

- **Airport**: Athens International Airport (ATH) is connected to the center by metro line 3 (about 40 minutes), 24-hour express buses, and suburban rail.
- **Metro**: Three lines; several stations display archaeological finds uncovered during construction. The system connects the airport, the center, and the port of Piraeus.
- **Buses, trolleybuses, and tram**: Cover areas the metro does not, including the coastal suburbs.
- **Tickets**: Integrated 90-minute tickets and daily passes cover all urban modes; airport services require a separate premium ticket.
- **Taxis**: Inexpensive by European standards; yellow cabs are metered, and taxi-hailing apps are widely used.
- **Ferries**: Depart from Piraeus to the Saronic and Aegean islands.
- **Walking**: The central archaeological sites are connected by a pedestrian promenade (Dionysiou Areopagitou).

## Budget Tips

- Athens is affordable by European capital standards: budget travelers can manage on 50–70 EUR per day, mid-range travelers on 100–150 EUR per day.
- A combined archaeological sites ticket covers the Acropolis and several other sites over five days and is worthwhile for multiple visits.
- From November to March, admission to major archaeological sites is reduced, and free-admission days occur on certain dates, including the first Sunday of winter months.
- Souvlaki shops provide filling meals for a few euros.
- Central Athens is compact enough to explore on foot, keeping transport costs minimal.

## Best Time to Visit

- **April–June and September–October** are ideal: warm, sunny, and less crowded than midsummer.
- **July–August** regularly exceeds 35°C; heat waves can force midday closures of archaeological sites.
- **Winter (November–March)** is mild (10–15°C) with some rain; sites are uncrowded and cheaper, though island ferry schedules are reduced.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Two to three days is the ideal length for the major ancient sites, the Acropolis Museum, and the central neighborhoods. Four to five days allows time for the Athens Riviera, a day trip to Cape Sounion (Temple of Poseidon), or a nearby island such as Aegina or Hydra.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Carry water and sun protection at archaeological sites; shade is minimal.
- Wear sturdy shoes; ancient marble surfaces are worn smooth and can be slippery.
- Validate public transport tickets before boarding.
- Pickpocketing occurs on metro line 1 and around Monastiraki; remain attentive.
- Sites stop admitting visitors 30 minutes before closing, and opening hours shorten significantly in winter.
- Tipping is customary but modest: round up or leave 5–10%.
- Tap water is safe to drink in Athens, though not on all Greek islands.
