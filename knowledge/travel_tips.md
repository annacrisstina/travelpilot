# Travel Tips

## Planning Ahead

- Book flights and accommodation several weeks to months in advance for the best prices, especially for high-season travel.
- Check passport validity: many countries require at least six months of validity beyond the travel dates.
- Verify visa requirements early; some visas take weeks to process, while others are available on arrival or electronically.
- Research public holidays and major events at the destination, which affect prices, crowds, and opening hours.
- Save offline copies of maps, reservations, and key documents in case of poor connectivity.
- Register travel plans with your country's foreign ministry service where available.

## Safety

- Research common risks for the specific destination rather than relying on general impressions.
- Keep valuables distributed rather than in one bag or pocket, and use hotel safes where available.
- Remain alert in crowded areas, transit hubs, and tourist sites, where pickpocketing is most common.
- Share an itinerary with someone at home and check in periodically.
- Avoid poorly lit or deserted areas at night in unfamiliar cities.

## Travel Insurance

- Travel insurance typically covers medical emergencies, trip cancellation, lost baggage, and delays; coverage levels vary widely.
- Medical evacuation coverage is important for remote destinations or activities such as trekking and diving.
- Standard policies often exclude high-risk activities, pre-existing conditions, and incidents involving alcohol; read exclusions carefully.
- Keep receipts and documentation for any claim, including police reports for theft.
- Some credit cards include limited travel insurance; verify what is actually covered before relying on it.

## Currency and Payments

- Notify banks of travel plans if required, and confirm foreign transaction fees on cards.
- Withdraw local currency from bank-affiliated ATMs rather than exchanging cash at airports or street kiosks, which offer poor rates.
- When a card terminal offers to charge in your home currency (dynamic currency conversion), decline and pay in local currency; the local rate is almost always better.
- Carry a small amount of cash for markets, small vendors, and emergencies, even in card-dominant countries.
- Keep a backup card stored separately from the primary wallet.

## Internet and Connectivity

- eSIMs allow data plans to be purchased and activated before arrival in most countries; physical local SIM cards are often cheaper for longer stays.
- International roaming is convenient but usually the most expensive option; check operator rates in advance.
- Public Wi-Fi is widespread but insecure; avoid banking or sensitive logins on open networks, or use a VPN.
- Download offline maps and translation language packs before departure.

## Local Etiquette

- Learn basic greetings and polite phrases in the local language; the effort is broadly appreciated.
- Research norms around tipping, dress, punctuality, and public behavior, which differ significantly between regions.
- Observe dress codes at religious sites, which commonly require covered shoulders and knees.
- Ask permission before photographing individuals, and observe photography restrictions at religious and government sites.

## Emergency Information

- Learn the local emergency number: 112 works across the European Union and in many other countries; 911 serves the United States and Canada; other countries use their own numbers.
- Record the address and phone number of your country's nearest embassy or consulate.
- Keep a physical and digital copy of your passport, insurance policy number, and emergency contacts.
- In case of passport loss, report to local police and contact the embassy for an emergency travel document.
- Know the location of a reputable hospital or clinic in the destination city.

## Sustainable Travel

- Direct flights produce fewer emissions than connecting itineraries; trains are typically the lowest-emission option for medium distances.
- Spend money with locally owned accommodations, restaurants, and guides to keep economic benefit in the community.
- Reduce single-use plastics by carrying a refillable water bottle where tap water is safe.
- Respect wildlife: avoid attractions involving captive animal performances or close contact with wild animals.
- Follow established trails, waste rules, and visitor limits at natural and heritage sites.
- Consider traveling in shoulder seasons to reduce pressure on overcrowded destinations.
