# London

## Overview

London is the capital of the United Kingdom and one of the world's most influential cities in finance, culture, and history. With a population of around 9 million, it is Western Europe's largest city. London's history spans nearly 2,000 years, from Roman Londinium through its role as the center of the British Empire to its current status as a global, multicultural metropolis. The city offers world-class free museums, royal landmarks, extensive parks, and distinct neighborhoods ranging from historic Westminster to creative East London.

## Top Attractions

- **British Museum**: One of the world's great museums, with collections spanning human history. Entry is free.
- **Tower of London**: A historic castle housing the Crown Jewels; tours led by Yeoman Warders are included with admission.
- **Westminster Abbey and Houses of Parliament**: The coronation church and the seat of UK government, with the Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben) adjacent.
- **Buckingham Palace**: The monarch's official residence; the Changing of the Guard ceremony is free to watch on scheduled days.
- **National Gallery and Tate Modern**: Major free art museums covering classical and modern art respectively.
- **London Eye**: A large observation wheel on the South Bank with panoramic city views.
- **St Paul's Cathedral**: Christopher Wren's Baroque masterpiece with a climbable dome.
- **South Bank and Borough Market**: A riverside walk connecting cultural venues, and a renowned food market near London Bridge.

## Local Cuisine

- **Traditional dishes**: Fish and chips, Sunday roast, full English breakfast, and savory pies.
- **Afternoon tea**: Served at hotels and tearooms, ranging from affordable to luxury.
- **Global cuisine**: London is one of the world's most diverse food cities, with notable Indian, Chinese, Middle Eastern, and West African food scenes.
- **Markets**: Borough Market, Camden Market, and Maltby Street Market offer varied street food.
- **Pub culture**: Pubs serve food and function as social institutions; ordering happens at the bar rather than at the table.

## Transportation

- **Airports**: Heathrow (LHR) is the main hub, connected by the Elizabeth Line, Piccadilly Line, and Heathrow Express. Gatwick (LGW), Stansted (STN), Luton (LTN), and City (LCY) also serve the city.
- **Underground (the Tube)**: 11 lines covering most of the city; service runs approximately 05:00–00:30, with Night Tube service on some lines on weekends.
- **Contactless payment**: Tap in and out with a contactless bank card or phone; fares are capped daily. An Oyster card is an alternative; paper tickets are more expensive.
- **Buses**: An extensive 24-hour network; cash is not accepted on board.
- **Trains**: National Rail, the Elizabeth Line, and the Overground serve outer areas and day-trip destinations.
- **Taxis and ride-hailing**: Black cabs can be hailed on the street; Uber and Bolt operate throughout.
- **Cycling**: Santander Cycles is the public bike-share scheme.

## Budget Tips

- Budget travelers should plan 70–100 GBP per day; mid-range travelers 150–250 GBP including accommodation. London is one of Europe's most expensive cities, and accommodation is the largest cost.
- Major museums (British Museum, National Gallery, Tate galleries, Natural History Museum, V&A) are free.
- Use contactless payment on transport; daily fare caps limit spending automatically.
- Supermarket meal deals and market street food reduce food costs substantially.
- Discounted theatre tickets are available through official day-seat lotteries and the TKTS booth in Leicester Square.
- Walking between central sights is often faster and cheaper than short Tube journeys.

## Best Time to Visit

- **Late spring (May–June)** and **early autumn (September)** offer the best balance of weather and crowds.
- **Summer (July–August)** brings the warmest weather (typically 20–25°C) along with peak crowds and prices.
- **Winter (November–February)** is cold, damp, and dark early, but offers lower prices; December is busy with holiday events.
- Rain is possible year-round, and conditions change quickly.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Four to five days is the ideal length for a first visit, covering Westminster, the City of London, the South Bank, two or three major museums, and one or two neighborhoods such as Notting Hill or Shoreditch. Additional days allow trips to Greenwich, Hampton Court, or Windsor.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Stand on the right on escalators; this rule is strictly observed.
- Card payments are accepted virtually everywhere; cash is rarely needed.
- Book popular attractions such as the Tower of London and the London Eye online for discounts and shorter queues.
- The UK uses type G electrical plugs (230V).
- Tipping: 10–12.5% in restaurants if a service charge is not already included; not expected in pubs.
- Free tap water is available on request in restaurants.
- Check for rail strikes or planned engineering works before relying on weekend train travel.
