# Amsterdam

## Overview

Amsterdam is the capital of the Netherlands, a city of approximately 900,000 residents known for its 17th-century canal ring (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), cycling culture, and world-class art museums. The city was a global trading power during the Dutch Golden Age, and its historic center retains over 1,500 bridges and thousands of gabled canal houses. Amsterdam is compact, flat, and highly navigable, with a well-integrated public transport system. English is spoken almost universally.

## Top Attractions

- **Rijksmuseum**: The national museum of the Netherlands, featuring Rembrandt's Night Watch and works by Vermeer.
- **Van Gogh Museum**: The world's largest Van Gogh collection. Advance online booking is mandatory; tickets often sell out days ahead.
- **Anne Frank House**: The preserved hiding place of Anne Frank. Tickets are released online six weeks in advance and sell out quickly.
- **Canal Ring (Grachtengordel)**: Best experienced on foot or by canal cruise; the adjacent Jordaan district is known for narrow streets and cafés.
- **Vondelpark**: The city's largest and most popular park.
- **Royal Palace and Dam Square**: The historic ceremonial center of the city.
- **NEMO Science Museum and the National Maritime Museum**: Located in the eastern docklands.
- **Day trips**: Zaanse Schans (windmills), Keukenhof gardens (spring only), Haarlem, and Utrecht, are easily reached by train or bus.

## Local Cuisine

- **Dutch snacks**: Stroopwafels, bitterballen (fried meat croquettes), raw herring from street stalls, and fries with sauces.
- **Cheese**: Gouda and Edam varieties sold at dedicated cheese shops and markets.
- **Indonesian cuisine**: A legacy of colonial history; rijsttafel (rice table) is a multi-dish Indonesian meal widely available.
- **Brown cafés**: Traditional Dutch pubs serving beer and simple food in historic interiors.
- **Pancakes**: Large Dutch pannenkoeken and small poffertjes are common casual meals.

## Transportation

- **Airport**: Schiphol (AMS) is a major international hub, connected to Amsterdam Centraal by direct train in 15–20 minutes.
- **Trams, buses, and metro**: Operated by GVB; trams are the most useful mode for visitors. Contactless bank card payment (OVpay) works by tapping in and out.
- **Cycling**: The dominant local transport mode. Rental shops are widespread and dedicated bike lanes cover the entire city. Follow traffic rules and lock bikes securely.
- **Trains**: NS national rail connects Amsterdam to all major Dutch cities at high frequency.
- **Ferries**: Free ferries cross the IJ river to Amsterdam Noord from behind Centraal station.
- **Walking**: The center is compact; most major sights are within a 30-minute walk of each other.
- **Taxis**: Expensive; use official ranks or apps. Uber operates in the city.

## Budget Tips

- Budget travelers should plan 70–100 EUR per day; mid-range travelers 150–250 EUR per day. Accommodation is expensive, and booking early matters more than in most European cities.
- The I amsterdam City Card covers many museums and public transport.
- Supermarkets (such as Albert Heijn) and street markets (Albert Cuypmarkt) offer inexpensive food options.
- Staying in Haarlem, Zaandam, or Amsterdam Noord offers lower rates with fast connections to the center.
- Renting a bike is cheaper than repeated transit tickets for stays of several days.
- Amsterdam's tourist tax is among Europe's highest and is added per night to accommodation bills.

## Best Time to Visit

- **April–May** brings tulip season (Keukenhof runs roughly late March to mid-May) and King's Day (April 27), the city's largest festival.
- **June–August** offers the warmest weather (typically 18–24°C) and peak crowds.
- **September–October** is quieter with mild weather.
- **Winter (November–February)** is cold, damp, and dark, but museums are uncrowded and prices lower.
- Rain is possible year-round; a rain jacket is advisable in any season.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Two to three days is sufficient for the major museums, the canal ring, and the Jordaan. Four to five days is ideal if adding day trips to Zaanse Schans, Haarlem, Utrecht, or Keukenhof in season.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Book the Anne Frank House and Van Gogh Museum well in advance; walk-up entry is generally not possible.
- Never walk in bike lanes (usually marked in red); cyclists have priority and move quickly.
- Museum visits are quietest at opening time or in the late afternoon.
- Cannabis is tolerated only in licensed coffeeshops; public consumption is restricted in much of the center.
- Card payments are standard; some businesses do not accept cash, and some accept only debit cards rather than credit cards.
- Canal edges often lack railings; exercise caution at night.
