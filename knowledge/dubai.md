# Dubai

## Overview

Dubai is the most populous city in the United Arab Emirates and the capital of the Emirate of Dubai, one of the seven emirates of the federation. Home to about 3.7 million people, the city has transformed within decades from a Gulf trading port into a global hub for aviation, tourism, finance, and trade. Dubai is known for record-setting architecture, including the Burj Khalifa, large-scale shopping malls, artificial islands, and desert landscapes. The population is predominantly expatriate, and English is used almost universally alongside Arabic. The city is modern and considered very safe, while local laws and customs reflect its Islamic context.

## Top Attractions

- **Burj Khalifa**: The world's tallest building at 828 meters; observation decks on floors 124–125 and 148 require timed tickets, with sunset slots priced highest.
- **Dubai Mall and Dubai Fountain**: One of the world's largest malls, adjacent to the Burj Khalifa; fountain shows run in the evenings and are free to watch.
- **Dubai Marina and JBR Beach**: A high-rise waterfront district with a beach promenade.
- **Palm Jumeirah**: The artificial palm-shaped island, home to the Atlantis resort and The View observation deck.
- **Old Dubai (Deira and Bur Dubai)**: The Gold and Spice Souks, the Al Fahidi historic district, and abra (wooden boat) crossings of Dubai Creek.
- **Dubai Frame**: A 150-meter picture-frame structure with views contrasting old and new Dubai.
- **Desert safaris**: Organized excursions for dune driving, camel rides, and dinner camps outside the city.
- **Museum of the Future and Burj Al Arab**: Landmark contemporary architecture; the Burj Al Arab is visitable via paid tours or restaurant reservations.

## Local Cuisine

- **Emirati dishes**: Machboos (spiced rice with meat), harees (wheat and meat porridge), and luqaimat (sweet dumplings), served at heritage restaurants.
- **Middle Eastern staples**: Shawarma, falafel, hummus, and grilled meats are inexpensive and widely available.
- **South Asian food**: Reflecting the city's demographics, Indian and Pakistani restaurants in Deira, Bur Dubai, and Karama offer excellent value.
- **International dining**: Hotel restaurants host high-end international cuisine; celebrity-chef establishments are concentrated in Downtown and the Marina.
- **Karak chai**: Sweet spiced tea sold cheaply at cafeterias throughout the city.

Alcohol is served only in licensed venues, primarily within hotels, and is heavily taxed.

## Transportation

- **Airport**: Dubai International (DXB) is one of the world's busiest international airports, connected to the city by two metro-served terminals, buses, and taxis.
- **Metro**: Two driverless lines (Red and Green) run elevated through the main corridors; the Red Line connects the airport, Downtown, the Marina, and Expo City. Gold Class and women-and-children cars are designated.
- **Nol card**: A rechargeable card required for metro, trams, and buses.
- **Taxis and ride-hailing**: Metered taxis are plentiful and moderately priced; Uber and the local app Careem operate citywide.
- **Tram and monorail**: The Dubai Tram serves the Marina area; a monorail runs along Palm Jumeirah.
- **Walking**: Practical only within districts and in cooler months; the city is car-oriented and distances between areas are large.
- **Abras**: Traditional boats cross Dubai Creek for a nominal fare.

## Budget Tips

- Budget travelers can manage on 250–400 AED per day; mid-range travelers should plan 500–1,000 AED per day including accommodation.
- Visit between May and September for sharply discounted hotel rates, accepting extreme heat.
- Cafeterias and South Asian restaurants in older districts serve full meals for 10–25 AED.
- Free activities include the Dubai Fountain shows, JBR and Kite Beach, the Al Fahidi district, and mall browsing.
- Book Burj Khalifa tickets online in advance; standard (non-prime) time slots cost significantly less.
- Use the metro instead of taxis along the Red Line corridor to save substantially.

## Best Time to Visit

- **November–March** is the high season, with warm, sunny days (24–30°C) suited to beaches and outdoor activities.
- **April and October** are transitional, hot but manageable.
- **May–September** brings extreme heat (frequently 40–45°C) and humidity; life moves indoors, and hotel prices drop substantially.
- During Ramadan (dates shift annually), many restaurants adjust daytime service and hours; evenings are lively after iftar.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Three to five days is the ideal length for Dubai. Three days covers Downtown and the Burj Khalifa, Old Dubai and the souks, and the Marina or beaches; four to five days adds a desert safari, Palm Jumeirah, and either a theme park or a day trip to Abu Dhabi (about 90 minutes away).

## Practical Travel Tips

- Dress modestly in malls, souks, and public areas (shoulders and knees covered); swimwear is acceptable only at beaches and pools.
- Public displays of affection beyond holding hands can cause offense; public intoxication is an offense.
- Photographing people, particularly women, and certain government buildings without permission is prohibited.
- The weekend is Saturday–Sunday; Friday midday prayers affect some opening hours.
- Taxis and card payments are safe and standard; tipping 10% or rounding up is customary but not mandatory.
- Stay hydrated and plan indoor activities for midday, even in winter.
- Check Ramadan dates when planning, and respect fasting norms during daylight hours in that period.
