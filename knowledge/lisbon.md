# Lisbon

## Overview

Lisbon is the capital of Portugal and one of Europe's oldest cities, situated on the north bank of the Tagus River estuary near the Atlantic coast. The city has a population of around 550,000, with about 2.9 million in the metropolitan area. Built across steep hills, Lisbon is known for its historic tram network, tiled building facades (azulejos), viewpoints (miradouros), and its role in the Age of Discoveries. The 1755 earthquake destroyed much of the city, leading to the planned rebuilding of the downtown Baixa district. Lisbon combines relatively low costs by Western European standards with a mild climate and growing international popularity.

## Top Attractions

- **Belém district**: Home to the Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower, both UNESCO World Heritage Sites, plus the Monument to the Discoveries.
- **Alfama**: The oldest district, a maze of narrow streets below São Jorge Castle, known for fado music venues.
- **São Jorge Castle**: A hilltop Moorish castle with panoramic city views.
- **Tram 28**: A historic tram route crossing the city's most scenic districts; heavily used by tourists, so board early in the day.
- **Praça do Comércio and Baixa**: The grand riverfront square and the rebuilt downtown grid.
- **Miradouros**: Viewpoints such as Senhora do Monte, Portas do Sol, and São Pedro de Alcântara.
- **National Tile Museum (Museu Nacional do Azulejo)**: Dedicated to Portugal's ceramic tile tradition.
- **Day trips**: Sintra's palaces (Pena Palace, Quinta da Regaleira) and the coastal town of Cascais, both reachable by direct train.

## Local Cuisine

- **Pastéis de nata**: Custard tarts; the historic bakery in Belém is the best-known producer.
- **Bacalhau**: Salted cod, prepared in numerous traditional ways.
- **Grilled sardines**: Especially popular during the June festivities of Santo António.
- **Bifana**: A marinated pork sandwich, a common inexpensive meal.
- **Seafood**: Clams (amêijoas à Bulhão Pato), octopus, and shellfish are staples.
- **Ginjinha**: A cherry liqueur served in small traditional bars around Rossio.

Appetizers (couvert) placed on restaurant tables are charged if eaten; they can be declined without charge.

## Transportation

- **Airport**: Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS) lies within the city, connected by the metro's red line and airport buses.
- **Metro**: Four lines covering central areas; clean and efficient.
- **Trams and funiculars**: Historic trams (including line 28) and funiculars handle the hills as part of the Carris public transport network.
- **Tickets**: The rechargeable Viva Viagem card works across metro, trams, buses, ferries, and suburban trains. A 24-hour pass covers unlimited urban travel.
- **Trains**: Suburban lines run from Rossio station (to Sintra) and Cais do Sodré (to Cascais).
- **Walking**: Central Lisbon is walkable but steep; comfortable shoes are essential on the traditional cobblestone pavements (calçada), which are slippery when wet.
- **Taxis and ride-hailing**: Inexpensive by European standards; Uber and Bolt are widely used.

## Budget Tips

- Lisbon is one of Western Europe's more affordable capitals: budget travelers can manage on 50–75 EUR per day, mid-range travelers on 100–160 EUR per day.
- The Lisboa Card combines public transport and museum entry and includes the train lines to Sintra and Cascais.
- Lunch specials (prato do dia) at local tascas typically cost 8–12 EUR.
- Many museums are free on the first Sunday of the month.
- Viewpoints, Alfama walks, and Belém's exterior sights are free.
- Buy pastries and coffee at counters rather than tourist-area terraces.

## Best Time to Visit

- **March–May and September–October** offer warm, sunny weather (18–25°C) without peak crowds.
- **June** features the city's largest festivals, especially Santo António (June 12–13).
- **July–August** is hot and crowded, though Atlantic breezes moderate temperatures compared with inland Iberia.
- **Winter (November–February)** is mild (10–15°C) and rainy but uncrowded; attractions remain fully open.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Three days is sufficient for central Lisbon and Belém. Four to five days is ideal, allowing a full-day trip to Sintra and a half-day in Cascais or at the beaches of the Costa da Caparica.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Wear shoes with good grip; polished cobblestones are genuinely slippery, especially downhill and in rain.
- Pickpocketing occurs on tram 28 and in crowded tourist areas.
- Book Pena Palace tickets online and arrive early; the Sintra sites are extremely busy midday.
- Politely decline unwanted table appetizers to avoid couvert charges.
- Individuals may offer drugs to tourists in the Baixa area; ignore them and walk on.
- Tipping is modest: rounding up or 5–10% for good service.
- Portuguese is the local language; English is widely spoken in tourist areas, and basic Portuguese greetings are appreciated.
