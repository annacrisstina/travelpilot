# Transportation

## Flights

- Book international flights roughly one to four months in advance; last-minute fares are usually the most expensive.
- Price comparison sites show the market, but booking directly with the airline simplifies changes and problem resolution.
- Low-cost carriers charge separately for seat selection, checked and often cabin baggage, and check-in services; compare the total price, not the headline fare.
- Direct flights cost more but eliminate missed-connection risk; when connecting, allow at least 90 minutes for international transfers, more if changing terminals or clearing immigration.
- Airlines are subject to passenger-rights rules in some jurisdictions (for example, EU Regulation 261/2004 provides compensation for certain delays and cancellations on EU-related flights).
- Check in online and verify baggage allowances before arriving at the airport.

## Trains

- Rail is often the most practical option for intercity distances of roughly 200–800 km, with city-center-to-city-center service and no airport procedures.
- High-speed networks (France, Spain, Italy, Germany, Japan, South Korea, China) require reservations on most services; book early for the lowest fares.
- Rail passes (such as Eurail/Interrail or the Japan Rail Pass) pay off only with frequent long-distance travel; calculate against point-to-point tickets first.
- Regional and commuter trains usually sell fixed-price tickets that need no advance purchase; some countries require validating tickets before boarding.
- Overnight trains can substitute for a hotel night on long routes.

## Public Transportation

- Metro, tram, and bus networks are the cheapest way to move within cities; most major systems offer day passes, multi-ride cards, or automatic fare capping.
- Contactless bank-card payment is increasingly standard (London, Amsterdam, New York, and others); other cities use rechargeable cards such as Suica/Pasmo (Tokyo), T-money (Seoul), Navigo (Paris), or Nol (Dubai).
- Validate paper tickets where required; inspections carry fines regardless of intent.
- Services in many cities stop between roughly midnight and 05:00; check night bus availability.
- Transit is also where pickpocketing is most common; keep bags closed and phones secure.

## Ride Sharing

- Ride-hailing apps (Uber, Bolt, Lyft, Grab, Careem, Kakao T, depending on region) provide fixed upfront pricing and remove language and cash barriers.
- Availability varies by country due to local regulation; in some cities apps dispatch licensed taxis rather than private drivers.
- Verify the car's plate and driver identity against the app before entering.
- Surge pricing raises fares during peak demand, bad weather, and major events; comparing with a metered taxi can pay off.
- Airport pickups often incur surcharges or use designated zones.

## Car Rental

- A car adds value for rural regions, road trips, and areas with sparse transit, but is a liability in dense cities due to traffic, parking costs, and restricted zones (such as Italian ZTLs or urban congestion charges).
- Most rentals require a credit card for the deposit; an International Driving Permit is required or recommended in many countries, including Japan and Greece for many license holders.
- Inspect and photograph the vehicle at pickup, and clarify fuel policy, mileage limits, and cross-border rules.
- Basic insurance is usually included; excess-reduction coverage can be purchased from the rental company or cheaper third-party providers.
- Manual transmissions are the default in Europe; automatic vehicles cost more and should be reserved early.

## Walking

- Walking is free, reliable, and often the fastest option for distances under two kilometers in dense city centers.
- Historic centers such as Rome, Lisbon, and Amsterdam are best experienced on foot; several are partially closed to cars.
- Comfortable, broken-in footwear matters more than any other packing decision for city trips; expect 10,000–20,000 steps per sightseeing day.
- Observe local traffic norms: cyclists have priority in Dutch bike lanes, traffic drives on the left in the UK and Japan, and jaywalking is fined in some countries.

## Choosing the Best Transportation Option

General guidance by distance and context:

- **Under 2 km**: walk, unless weather or terrain prevents it.
- **2–10 km (within a city)**: public transit by default; ride-hailing for late nights, luggage, or groups.
- **10–200 km**: regional trains or buses; rental car for rural areas.
- **200–800 km**: high-speed rail where available; otherwise flights or long-distance buses.
- **Over 800 km**: flights are usually fastest; overnight trains are an alternative on served routes.

Other factors: total door-to-door time (including airport procedures), luggage, number of travelers (cars and taxis become competitive for groups of three or four), budget, and environmental impact, where rail generally performs best.
