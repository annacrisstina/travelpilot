# Accommodation

## Hotels

- Hotels offer private rooms with daily housekeeping, reception services, and standardized quality levels indicated by star ratings, though rating criteria vary by country.
- **Budget hotels (1–2 stars)** provide basic private rooms, sometimes with shared bathrooms in older European properties.
- **Mid-range hotels (3 stars)** typically include private bathrooms, breakfast options, and 24-hour reception; business hotel chains in Japan and South Korea offer small but efficient and reliable rooms in this tier.
- **Upscale hotels (4–5 stars)** add restaurants, fitness facilities, concierge services, and higher space and service standards.
- Typical city pricing: 60–120 EUR/USD per night for budget, 100–250 for mid-range, and 250 upward for upscale properties in major Western cities, with wide seasonal variation.
- Hotels suit travelers prioritizing predictability, service, and no self-management of the stay.

## Hostels

- Hostels sell beds in shared dormitories (typically 4–12 beds) as well as private rooms, with shared kitchens and common areas.
- Dorm beds cost roughly 20–50 EUR/USD in Western European capitals and less elsewhere, making hostels the cheapest commercial lodging.
- Modern hostels frequently offer women-only dorms, privacy curtains, individual lockers, and power outlets per bed; standards have risen substantially over the past decade.
- Hostels are social by design and remain the default choice for solo and young travelers, though age limits are rare and private rooms attract couples and older travelers.
- Evaluate reviews for cleanliness, security (lockers, keycard access), and noise; location quality varies as much as in hotels.

## Apartments

- Short-term rental apartments (via Airbnb, Booking.com, Vrbo, and local agencies) provide kitchens, laundry, and more space than hotel rooms at comparable or lower prices for stays of several nights.
- They suit families, groups, and stays of four nights or more, where self-catering and space deliver the greatest value.
- Total cost includes cleaning fees and service charges, which penalize short stays; compare the all-in price per night.
- Regulation is tightening in many cities (registration numbers required in Amsterdam, Barcelona, New York, and others); book only listed, licensed properties.
- Expect less service than hotels: no daily cleaning, self check-in, and variable host responsiveness.
- Verify practical details in reviews: heating or air conditioning, Wi-Fi reliability, noise, and accuracy of photos.

## Resorts

- Resorts are self-contained properties combining lodging, dining, pools, and activities, common in beach, island, and desert destinations such as Dubai, and in ski regions.
- **All-inclusive** pricing covers meals and often drinks and activities; it simplifies budgeting but reduces incentive to explore local restaurants and towns.
- Resort quality is defined by facilities as much as rooms; day passes at some properties allow access without staying.
- Location trade-off: resorts are frequently distant from city centers, making them poor bases for sightseeing-focused trips.
- Check what is genuinely included (motorized water sports, premium dining, and spa services are common exclusions) and applicable resort fees, which are widespread in the United States.

## Booking Tips

- Book early for high season, festivals, and major events; in cities such as Amsterdam and during periods such as cherry-blossom season in Tokyo, availability tightens months ahead.
- Compare aggregator prices with the property's own website; direct bookings sometimes carry better cancellation terms, loyalty benefits, or room upgrades.
- Prefer free-cancellation rates when plans could change; the premium over non-refundable rates functions as insurance.
- Read recent reviews (past 6–12 months) and look for repeated specific complaints rather than isolated ones.
- Confirm the total price: taxes, city tourist taxes (often charged per person per night at the property), resort fees, and cleaning fees can add substantially to headline rates.
- Contact the property directly for late arrivals, early check-ins, or special requirements; note standard check-in (14:00–15:00) and check-out (10:00–12:00) windows.

## Choosing the Right Location

- Prioritize proximity to a metro or transit station over proximity to a single attraction; a well-connected location reaches the whole city.
- Central neighborhoods cost more but save daily transport time and money; quiet residential districts one or two transit stops out often offer the best value.
- Match the neighborhood to the trip: nightlife districts are loud at night; business districts empty on weekends; station areas are convenient but sometimes charmless.
- For one-night or transit stays, staying near the station or airport is rational; for longer stays, character and daily convenience matter more.
- Research safety at the neighborhood level using recent traveler reviews rather than citywide reputations.
- Confirm the arrival route from the airport before booking — a cheap room with a difficult late-night connection is a poor trade.
