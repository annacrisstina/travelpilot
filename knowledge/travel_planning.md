# Travel Planning

## Choosing a Destination

Key factors to weigh when selecting a destination:

- **Time available**: long-haul destinations rarely justify trips under a week once travel time and jet lag are counted.
- **Season**: research the destination's climate calendar — high season brings the best weather and the biggest crowds and prices; shoulder seasons often offer the best balance.
- **Budget fit**: daily costs vary several-fold between, for example, Lisbon or Athens and London, New York, or Dubai.
- **Interests**: match the destination to priorities such as museums, food, beaches, hiking, or nightlife rather than general reputation.
- **Practicalities**: visa requirements, flight connections, language barriers, and safety advisories.
- **Travel companions**: energy levels, mobility, and interests of the whole group constrain realistic choices.

## Setting a Budget

- Establish the total available amount first, then divide it across categories: transport to the destination, accommodation, daily spending (food, local transport, activities), and a 10–15% contingency.
- Research realistic daily costs for the specific destination and travel style; guidebook and cost-of-living data are more reliable than assumptions.
- Account for pre-trip costs often forgotten: insurance, visas, vaccinations, gear, and airport transfers at home.
- Note fixed local costs such as city tourist taxes, attraction tickets, and connectivity.
- Decide spending priorities explicitly — for example, economizing on lodging to fund better dining — rather than overspending in every category.

## Creating an Itinerary

- Plan around two or three anchor activities per day at most; overpacked schedules fail in practice due to queues, distances, and fatigue.
- Group sights geographically to minimize crossing the city repeatedly.
- Check opening days early: many museums close on Mondays or Tuesdays, and religious sites restrict tourist visits during services.
- Book high-demand attractions (e.g., Sagrada Família, Anne Frank House, Vatican Museums, Ghibli Museum) as soon as dates are fixed; several sell out weeks ahead.
- Alternate intense sightseeing days with lighter ones, and leave unstructured time for discoveries.
- Plan day trips for the middle of a stay, not arrival or departure days.
- Keep the first day light to absorb arrival logistics and jet lag.

## Booking Transportation

- Book international flights one to four months ahead for typical routes; set fare alerts if dates are flexible.
- Compare the true door-to-door cost and time of alternatives — a cheap flight to a distant secondary airport can lose its advantage after transfers.
- For multi-city trips, price open-jaw flights (into one city, out of another) to avoid backtracking.
- Book high-speed rail early where dynamic pricing applies (France, Spain, Italy, Japan); regional trains rarely need advance purchase.
- Arrange the arrival airport transfer in advance if landing late at night.
- Record booking references offline and note check-in requirements, especially for low-cost carriers that charge for airport check-in.

## Booking Accommodation

- Prioritize location relative to transit and the planned itinerary; savings from a remote hotel are often consumed by transport time and cost.
- Book early for high season, festivals, and trade-fair periods, when entire cities sell out.
- Compare platform prices with the property's direct rate, which sometimes includes better terms.
- Read recent reviews for recurring specifics (noise, cleanliness, Wi-Fi reliability) rather than the headline score alone.
- Verify total price including taxes, cleaning fees, and city tourist taxes charged at the property.
- Prefer flexible cancellation terms when plans are not certain; the small premium functions as insurance.

## Preparing Travel Documents

- Check passport validity immediately upon deciding to travel: many countries require six months' validity beyond the stay, and renewal can take weeks.
- Determine visa or electronic authorization requirements (e.g., ESTA for the US, ETA for the UK, K-ETA for South Korea where applicable) and apply well ahead.
- Purchase travel insurance at booking time so cancellation coverage applies from that point.
- Prepare copies: physical and cloud-stored digital copies of passports, visas, insurance, and bookings.
- Verify driving documents (license, International Driving Permit) if renting a vehicle.
- Check vaccination requirements and health documentation for the destination.

## Pre-Departure Checklist

Two weeks before:

- Confirm all bookings and check passport, visas, and insurance documents.
- Arrange currency or cards, notify the bank if required, and set up an eSIM or roaming plan.
- Book airport transfers and any remaining timed attraction tickets.

A few days before:

- Check in for flights when the window opens; download boarding passes.
- Download offline maps, translation packs, and entertainment.
- Pack against a written checklist; weigh luggage against airline limits.
- Arrange home logistics: mail, plants, pets, and an itinerary copy for a contact person.

Departure day:

- Carry documents, medications, valuables, and chargers in hand luggage.
- Arrive at the airport 2–3 hours before international departures.
- Verify the terminal, and keep the first night's accommodation address accessible offline.
