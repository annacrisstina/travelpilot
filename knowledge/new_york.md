# New York City

## Overview

New York City is the largest city in the United States, with about 8.3 million residents across five boroughs: Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island. It is a global center of finance, media, art, and culture, and one of the most linguistically and ethnically diverse cities in the world. Most visitor activity concentrates in Manhattan and parts of Brooklyn. The city operates around the clock, with a 24-hour subway system, and offers an exceptional density of museums, performing arts venues, restaurants, and iconic architecture.

## Top Attractions

- **Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island**: Reached by ferry from Battery Park; crown access requires booking months ahead.
- **Central Park**: An 341-hectare (843-acre) park in the middle of Manhattan, free to enter.
- **Metropolitan Museum of Art**: One of the world's largest art museums, on the eastern edge of Central Park.
- **Empire State Building, Top of the Rock, and Summit One Vanderbilt**: Competing observation decks; Top of the Rock offers views that include the Empire State Building itself.
- **Times Square and Broadway**: The theater district; shows run most evenings with matinees on selected days.
- **9/11 Memorial and Museum**: Located at the World Trade Center site in Lower Manhattan.
- **Brooklyn Bridge**: Walkable between Lower Manhattan and Brooklyn; the Brooklyn side offers skyline views from DUMBO and Brooklyn Bridge Park.
- **High Line**: An elevated park built on a former rail line, running through Chelsea to Hudson Yards.
- **Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) and American Museum of Natural History**: Additional world-class museums.

## Local Cuisine

- **Pizza**: New York-style slices are an inexpensive staple, sold citywide.
- **Bagels**: Typically served with cream cheese or as breakfast sandwiches; a local institution.
- **Delis**: Pastrami on rye and other Jewish deli classics at historic establishments.
- **Global cuisine**: Chinatown, Koreatown, Jackson Heights (Indian, Tibetan, Latin American), and Flushing (regional Chinese) offer some of the best value in the city.
- **Street food**: Hot dog and halal carts are ubiquitous; food halls such as Chelsea Market aggregate vendors.
- **Fine dining**: The city has one of the world's largest concentrations of top-rated restaurants; reservations open weeks ahead.

## Transportation

- **Airports**: John F. Kennedy (JFK) is connected by the AirTrain plus subway or Long Island Rail Road; Newark (EWR) by AirTrain plus NJ Transit trains; LaGuardia (LGA) by buses only.
- **Subway**: A 24-hour system covering four boroughs. Pay by tapping a contactless card or phone (OMNY); fares are a flat rate per ride with a weekly spending cap.
- **Buses**: Cover crosstown routes and areas without subway access; the same fare system applies.
- **Taxis and ride-hailing**: Yellow cabs can be hailed on the street in Manhattan; Uber and Lyft operate throughout.
- **Ferries**: NYC Ferry routes along the East River; the Staten Island Ferry is free and passes the Statue of Liberty.
- **Walking**: Manhattan's grid makes navigation simple; walking is often faster than driving for short distances.

## Budget Tips

- New York is expensive: budget travelers should plan 120–180 USD per day; mid-range travelers 250–450 USD per day including accommodation, which is the dominant cost.
- Several museums offer pay-what-you-wish admission on set days or for New York State residents; check current policies before visiting.
- Broadway discounts are available through the TKTS booth, digital lotteries, and rush tickets.
- Pizza slices, bagels, food carts, and outer-borough restaurants keep food costs manageable.
- The free Staten Island Ferry substitutes for paid harbor cruises.
- Sales tax (8.875%) and tips are added to listed prices; budget accordingly.
- Staying in Long Island City, Williamsburg, or Jersey City can reduce lodging costs with short commutes.

## Best Time to Visit

- **April–June and September–early November** offer the most comfortable weather and are the most popular periods.
- **Summer (July–August)** is hot and humid (often 30–35°C), with free outdoor events but heavy crowds.
- **December** is festive and busy around the holidays, with high hotel prices.
- **January–February** is cold (often below 0°C) and is the cheapest period for accommodation.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Four to six days is the ideal length for a first visit: two to three days for Manhattan's major sights (Midtown, Central Park and museums, Lower Manhattan), one day for Brooklyn, and one day for a Broadway show, additional museums, or neighborhoods such as Harlem or the Village. A week allows a more relaxed pace and outer-borough exploration.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Tipping is expected: 18–20% in restaurants, 1–2 USD per drink at bars, and 15–20% for taxis.
- Listed prices exclude sales tax, which is added at checkout.
- Use OMNY contactless payment on the subway; no separate card purchase is needed.
- Express and local subway trains share lines; check whether a train stops at your station.
- Book the Statue of Liberty, observation decks, and popular restaurants well in advance.
- Streets are generally safe in visitor areas, but remain aware of belongings on the subway and in crowds.
- Museums are typically closed one day per week; verify hours before visiting.
