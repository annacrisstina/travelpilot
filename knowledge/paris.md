# Paris

## Overview

Paris is the capital of France and one of the world's leading destinations for art, culture, gastronomy, and fashion. The city proper has a population of about 2.1 million, with over 12 million in the metropolitan area. Paris is organized into 20 arrondissements (districts) spiraling outward from the center. The Seine River divides the city into the Right Bank and Left Bank. The city hosts some of the world's most visited museums and monuments, and its urban landscape is defined by Haussmann-era architecture, grand boulevards, and historic neighborhoods.

## Top Attractions

- **Eiffel Tower**: The city's most recognizable landmark, completed in 1889. Summit access requires advance booking during peak season.
- **Louvre Museum**: The world's largest art museum, home to the Mona Lisa and the Venus de Milo. Closed on Tuesdays; timed-entry tickets are recommended.
- **Notre-Dame Cathedral**: The Gothic cathedral on Île de la Cité, reopened to visitors in December 2024 following restoration after the 2019 fire.
- **Musée d'Orsay**: A major collection of Impressionist and Post-Impressionist art housed in a former railway station.
- **Arc de Triomphe and Champs-Élysées**: A monumental arch with a rooftop viewpoint at the top of the famous avenue.
- **Sacré-Cœur and Montmartre**: A hilltop basilica surrounded by a historic artists' quarter.
- **Sainte-Chapelle**: A 13th-century chapel known for its stained glass windows.
- **Palace of Versailles**: A former royal residence outside the city, reachable by RER C train; typically a half-day or full-day trip.

## Local Cuisine

French cuisine is central to the Paris experience:

- **Bakery items**: Baguettes, croissants, and pain au chocolat from local boulangeries.
- **Classic dishes**: Steak frites, duck confit, onion soup, coq au vin, and escargots.
- **Cheese and wine**: Fromageries and wine bars offer regional specialties.
- **Crêpes**: Available from street vendors and sit-down crêperies.
- **Pâtisserie**: Macarons, éclairs, and tarts from dedicated pastry shops.

Fixed-price lunch menus (formule or menu du jour) offer significantly better value than dinner at the same establishments.

## Transportation

- **Airports**: Charles de Gaulle (CDG) is the main international hub; Orly (ORY) serves many European and domestic routes. RER B trains and airport buses connect CDG to the center.
- **Metro**: 16 lines with dense coverage; trains run from approximately 05:30 to 01:15, later on weekends.
- **RER**: Regional express trains connecting the suburbs, airports, and Versailles.
- **Tickets**: The Navigo Easy card and weekly Navigo pass have largely replaced paper tickets. Single fares are charged per ride within the city.
- **Buses and trams**: Useful for surface travel and sightseeing along the way.
- **Vélib'**: A public bicycle-sharing system with extensive station coverage.
- **Taxis and ride-hailing**: Official taxis have fixed airport rates; Uber and Bolt operate widely.

## Budget Tips

- Budget travelers can expect 70–100 EUR per day; mid-range travelers 150–250 EUR per day including accommodation.
- Many national museums are free on the first Sunday of each month; the permanent collections of city-run museums are free year-round.
- EU residents under 26 enter many national museums free of charge.
- Picnics assembled from bakeries and markets are an economical alternative to restaurants.
- The Paris Museum Pass covers over 50 sites and can save money for intensive sightseeing.
- Book accommodation well in advance; prices rise sharply during fashion weeks and major events.

## Best Time to Visit

- **Spring (April–June)** and **early autumn (September–October)** offer mild weather and long daylight hours.
- **Summer (July–August)** is peak tourist season; some smaller businesses close in August.
- **Winter (November–February)** is cold and gray but offers lower prices and shorter museum queues, except during the holiday period in December.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Four to five days is the ideal length for a first visit. This allows one day for the Louvre and central Right Bank, one day for the Eiffel Tower and Left Bank, one day for Montmartre and other neighborhoods, and one day for the Musée d'Orsay and Île de la Cité. A fifth day accommodates a trip to Versailles.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Greet staff with "Bonjour" when entering shops; it is a strong social norm.
- Pickpocketing occurs on the metro and around major attractions; keep valuables secure.
- Most museums close one day per week, usually Monday or Tuesday; check schedules before planning each day.
- Restaurant service charges are included by law; additional tipping is optional (rounding up or 5–10%).
- Many shops close on Sundays, though tourist areas remain active.
- Tap water is safe; ask for "une carafe d'eau" for free water in restaurants.
