# Packing Guide

## Essential Documents

- Passport, valid for at least six months beyond travel dates for many destinations, with required blank pages.
- Visas or electronic travel authorizations (e.g., ESTA for the US, ETA for the UK, K-ETA for South Korea where applicable) arranged in advance.
- Printed and digital copies of the passport photo page, stored separately from the original.
- Travel insurance policy details and emergency contact numbers.
- Booking confirmations for flights, accommodation, and prepaid activities, available offline.
- Driving license and International Driving Permit if renting a vehicle.
- Any required vaccination certificates.
- One or two payment cards plus a small amount of cash, stored in separate places.

## Clothing

- Pack for layering rather than bulk: base layers, a warm mid-layer, and a waterproof outer shell cover most climates.
- Choose items that mix and match in a limited color palette; plan for roughly one week of clothing and launder on longer trips.
- Comfortable, broken-in walking shoes are essential; add one dressier option only if needed.
- Include modest clothing (covering shoulders and knees) for religious sites in Europe, the Middle East, and Asia.
- Add climate-specific items: sun hat and breathable fabrics for heat; hat, gloves, and insulated layers for cold; a compact rain jacket almost everywhere.
- Swimwear where relevant, including for hotel pools and hot springs.

## Electronics

- Phone and charger, plus a power bank for long sightseeing days.
- Universal plug adapter; check destination plug types and voltage (e.g., type G in the UK and Dubai; types A/B and 100V in Japan; types C/F in most of continental Europe).
- eSIM configured before departure, or an unlocked phone for local SIM cards.
- Optional: e-reader, noise-canceling headphones, camera, and a lightweight laptop or tablet if needed.
- A single multi-port USB charger reduces the number of adapters required.

## Health Items

- Prescription medications in original packaging, in carry-on luggage, with enough supply for the trip plus a margin; carry a copy of prescriptions and check destination rules, as some common medications are restricted in countries such as Japan and the UAE.
- Basic first-aid kit: pain relievers, plasters, blister care, antiseptic, anti-diarrheal medication, and oral rehydration salts.
- Sunscreen and insect repellent appropriate to the destination.
- Hand sanitizer and any personal hygiene items that may be hard to find locally.
- Glasses or contact lens supplies, plus a spare prescription.
- Any destination-specific requirements advised by a travel clinic (e.g., malaria prophylaxis, altitude medication).

## Travel Accessories

- Luggage suited to the trip: a carry-on-sized case or backpack suffices for most city trips and avoids checked-bag fees and waits.
- Packing cubes for organization and compression.
- Refillable water bottle, foldable day bag, and a compact umbrella.
- Travel document organizer or a slim anti-theft pouch worn under clothing in high-pickpocketing destinations.
- Luggage locks (TSA-approved for US travel) and a luggage tag.
- Sleep aids for long flights: eye mask, earplugs, and a neck pillow.
- A pen for arrival forms, and a few plastic bags for laundry and liquids.

## Country-Specific Considerations

- **Japan and South Korea**: Carry cash for small vendors; pack slip-on shoes for frequent shoe removal; small towels are useful as public restrooms may lack dryers.
- **United Arab Emirates**: Modest clothing for public areas; a layer for heavily air-conditioned interiors; note strict rules on medications containing codeine or similar substances.
- **United Kingdom and Northern Europe**: Waterproof jacket year-round; type G adapters for the UK.
- **Southern Europe in summer**: Sun protection, and clothing that meets church dress codes despite the heat.
- **United States**: Larger distances may mean more car travel; tipping culture makes small bills useful.

## Packing Tips

- Write a checklist and pack a day or two ahead, not on departure day.
- Roll or fold with packing cubes to save space; keep bags no more than about 80% full to allow for purchases.
- Carry-on essentials for checked-bag travelers: medications, valuables, electronics, documents, and one change of clothes in case checked luggage is delayed.
- Follow liquid rules for carry-on (containers up to 100 ml in most jurisdictions) and remove prohibited items before security.
- Weigh bags at home against airline limits, which are strict on low-cost carriers.
- Leave space for souvenirs, or pack a lightweight foldable bag for the return journey.
