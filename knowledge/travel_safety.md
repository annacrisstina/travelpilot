# Travel Safety

## Personal Safety

- Research the destination's specific risks before departure using official government travel advisories rather than anecdotes.
- Keep valuables minimal and distributed: one card and modest cash in a front pocket or zipped bag, backups locked in the accommodation.
- Stay alert in crowds, transit stations, and around major attractions, where opportunistic theft concentrates.
- At night, use well-lit main streets and licensed transport rather than walking through unfamiliar or deserted areas.
- Match alcohol consumption to the environment; intoxication is a factor in a large share of traveler incidents, and drink spiking occurs in nightlife districts worldwide — keep drinks in sight.
- Trust instincts: leaving a situation that feels wrong costs nothing.

## Health Precautions

- Consult a travel clinic four to eight weeks before departure for destination-specific vaccinations and advice (e.g., hepatitis A, typhoid, malaria prophylaxis where relevant).
- Carry prescription medications in original packaging with documentation, and verify they are legal at the destination.
- In regions where tap water is unsafe, drink bottled or treated water, avoid ice of unknown origin, and be cautious with raw foods.
- Use sun protection and stay hydrated in hot climates; heat exhaustion is a common and underestimated risk at exposed sites in summer.
- Prevent insect bites in mosquito-borne-disease regions with repellent, covering clothing, and nets where provided.
- Carry proof of travel health insurance; the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC/GHIC) covers state healthcare for eligible travelers within the EU but does not replace travel insurance.

## Emergency Contacts

- Learn the local emergency number before arrival: 112 across the European Union (and widely elsewhere), 911 in the United States and Canada, 999 in the United Kingdom, 110/119 in Japan (police/fire-ambulance), and 112 in South Korea for police.
- Save and write down the address and phone number of your country's nearest embassy or consulate.
- Store an ICE (In Case of Emergency) contact in your phone, accessible from the lock screen.
- Keep the accommodation's address in the local language for taxi drivers and emergencies.
- For lost passports: file a local police report, then contact the embassy for an emergency travel document.
- For lost or stolen cards: use the bank's app or hotline to freeze cards immediately.

## Common Tourist Scams

- **Taxi overcharging**: unmetered rides, "broken meters," or long routes. Use official ranks, insist on the meter, or use ride-hailing apps with fixed pricing.
- **Distraction theft**: someone spills something on you, asks you to sign a petition, or creates a commotion while an accomplice takes valuables.
- **Friendship bracelets and "free" gifts**: an item is placed on you, then payment is demanded.
- **Bar scams**: strangers invite travelers to a specific venue where an inflated bill is enforced.
- **Fake officials**: individuals posing as police request wallets or documents; ask for identification and offer to walk to a police station.
- **Rental damage claims**: photograph vehicles, scooters, and apartments at handover.
- **Overpriced or counterfeit tickets**: buy attraction tickets only from official websites or ticket offices.

The consistent defense is skepticism toward unsolicited approaches and refusal to be rushed into decisions.

## Safe Transportation

- Use licensed taxis or established ride-hailing apps; verify plate and driver against the app before entering.
- On overnight trains and buses, secure luggage to racks and keep valuables on your person.
- Wear seatbelts where available, and avoid road travel at night in countries with poor road-safety records.
- When renting scooters or motorbikes, wear a helmet, hold the required license, and confirm insurance coverage — motorbike accidents are a leading cause of serious tourist injury in Southeast Asia.
- On ferries and boats, note the location of life jackets and exits.

## Digital Security

- Avoid logging into banking or sensitive accounts on public Wi-Fi; use mobile data or a reputable VPN.
- Enable two-factor authentication before travel, and ensure it works without SMS if changing SIMs.
- Set a strong device passcode and enable remote-wipe features (Find My iPhone, Find My Device).
- Beware of juice-jacking claims aside, the practical risks are shoulder-surfing and device theft; keep phones off café table edges and out of back pockets.
- Back up photos and documents to cloud storage during the trip.
- Be cautious with public computers in hotels or internet cafés; never save passwords on them.

## Solo Travel Safety

- Share the itinerary with someone at home and agree on a check-in rhythm.
- Arrive in new cities during daylight when possible; navigating unfamiliar areas at night with luggage increases vulnerability.
- Book first-night accommodation in advance with verified reviews.
- Stay in social but secure accommodation (well-reviewed hostels or hotels with 24-hour reception) when meeting other travelers matters.
- Be measured about sharing personal details and real-time location publicly on social media.
- Women traveling solo may prefer women-only dorms, licensed transport at night, and researching destination-specific norms; overall, popular tourist destinations in Europe and East Asia have strong solo-travel safety records.
