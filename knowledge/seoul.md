# Seoul

## Overview

Seoul is the capital of South Korea, a metropolis of about 9.4 million people (over 25 million in the wider capital region) situated on the Han River. The city has served as Korea's capital for over 600 years, since the founding of the Joseon Dynasty in 1392. Modern Seoul is a global center of technology, entertainment, and design, and the source of the internationally influential Korean wave in music, film, and food. The city combines royal palaces, Buddhist temples, and traditional hanok neighborhoods with dense high-rise districts, an extensive subway system, and a 24-hour street-food and shopping culture. Seoul is considered very safe for visitors.

## Top Attractions

- **Gyeongbokgung Palace**: The largest of Seoul's five Joseon-era palaces; the changing of the royal guard ceremony takes place on scheduled days. Visitors wearing rented hanbok (traditional dress) enter free.
- **Bukchon Hanok Village**: A residential neighborhood of traditional Korean houses between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung palaces.
- **Changdeokgung Palace and Secret Garden**: A UNESCO World Heritage Site; the garden is visitable by guided tour.
- **Myeongdong**: The main downtown shopping district, known for cosmetics stores and street food.
- **N Seoul Tower (Namsan)**: An observation tower on Namsan mountain, reachable by cable car or hiking paths.
- **Insadong**: A street known for traditional crafts, tea houses, and galleries.
- **Hongdae and Gangnam**: Districts representing youth culture and modern commercial Seoul respectively.
- **Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP)**: A landmark building by Zaha Hadid, adjacent to 24-hour shopping complexes.
- **Day trips**: The DMZ (Demilitarized Zone) by organized tour, and the Korean Folk Village or Suwon's Hwaseong Fortress by public transport.

## Local Cuisine

- **Korean barbecue**: Grilled meats such as samgyeopsal (pork belly) and galbi (marinated ribs), cooked at the table.
- **Bibimbap**: Rice topped with vegetables, meat, egg, and gochujang chili paste.
- **Kimchi**: Fermented vegetables served with virtually every meal.
- **Street food**: Tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes), hotteok (sweet filled pancakes), and gimbap, widely available in markets such as Gwangjang.
- **Fried chicken and beer (chimaek)**: A popular social meal.
- **Banchan**: Small side dishes served free with meals and typically refillable.

Restaurant prices are moderate, and solo dining is increasingly common, though some barbecue restaurants require a minimum of two portions.

## Transportation

- **Airports**: Incheon International Airport (ICN), the main hub, is connected to the city by the AREX express and all-stop trains and by limousine buses. Gimpo Airport (GMP) handles domestic and short-haul international flights.
- **Subway**: One of the world's most extensive and efficient networks, with more than 20 lines, English signage, and phone connectivity throughout. Services run from roughly 05:30 to midnight.
- **T-money card**: A rechargeable card used on subways, buses, and taxis and at convenience stores; available at stations and convenience stores.
- **Buses**: Color-coded routes complement the subway; the T-money card provides free or discounted transfers.
- **Taxis**: Plentiful and moderately priced; the Kakao T app is the standard hailing service. International ride-hailing apps have limited presence.
- **Walking**: Individual districts are walkable, but the city is large and hilly in places; the subway is the practical way to move between areas.

## Budget Tips

- Budget travelers can manage on 60,000–90,000 KRW per day; mid-range travelers should plan 120,000–250,000 KRW per day including accommodation.
- Street food and local restaurants (5,000–12,000 KRW per meal) keep food costs low.
- Palaces charge small entry fees (about 3,000 KRW); an integrated ticket covers four palaces and the Jongmyo Shrine.
- Renting hanbok grants free palace entry and is a popular activity in itself.
- Convenience stores sell inexpensive meals, and free water is standard in restaurants.
- Guesthouses and business hotels offer good value compared with international chains.

## Best Time to Visit

- **Spring (April–May)**: Mild weather and cherry blossoms in early April.
- **Autumn (September–November)**: Clear skies, comfortable temperatures, and fall foliage; generally considered the best season.
- **Summer (June–August)**: Hot and humid, with a monsoon period in late June–July.
- **Winter (December–February)**: Cold and dry, often below 0°C, but with low prices and winter activities nearby.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Three to five days is the ideal length for Seoul. Three days covers the palaces, Bukchon, Myeongdong, and one or two modern districts; five days allows markets, Namsan, Hongdae and Gangnam at a comfortable pace, plus a DMZ tour or another day trip.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Google Maps has limited functionality in South Korea; use Naver Map or Kakao Map for navigation.
- Obtain a T-money card on arrival for all local transport.
- Card payments are accepted almost everywhere, including markets.
- Many restaurants use table-side call buttons to summon staff; this is normal practice.
- Tipping is not customary and is not expected.
- Shoes are removed in traditional restaurants with floor seating and in guesthouses.
- An eSIM or local SIM is inexpensive and recommended, as public Wi-Fi, while common, is not universal.
- Priority seating on the subway is strictly reserved for the elderly, pregnant, and disabled; leave it empty otherwise.
