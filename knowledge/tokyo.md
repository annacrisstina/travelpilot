# Tokyo

## Overview

Tokyo is the capital of Japan and the center of the world's most populous metropolitan area, home to over 37 million people in the greater region. The city serves as Japan's political, economic, and cultural hub. Tokyo consists of 23 special wards plus surrounding cities, each district with a distinct character — from the electronics quarter of Akihabara to the fashion centers of Shibuya and Harajuku and the traditional atmosphere of Asakusa. The city is exceptionally safe, clean, and punctual, with one of the world's most extensive public transportation networks, and it balances ultramodern architecture with preserved temples, shrines, and gardens.

## Top Attractions

- **Senso-ji Temple (Asakusa)**: Tokyo's oldest temple, approached via the Nakamise shopping street.
- **Meiji Shrine**: A major Shinto shrine set in a forested park adjacent to Harajuku.
- **Shibuya Crossing**: The famous scramble intersection, surrounded by shopping districts and observation decks such as Shibuya Sky.
- **Tokyo Skytree and Tokyo Tower**: Observation towers with panoramic city views.
- **Imperial Palace East Gardens**: The former Edo Castle grounds, free to enter.
- **Tsukiji Outer Market**: A street-food and seafood market (the wholesale auction moved to Toyosu).
- **Ueno Park**: Home to the Tokyo National Museum, several other museums, and a zoo.
- **Akihabara**: The center of electronics, anime, and gaming culture.
- **Shinjuku**: Skyscrapers, the free Tokyo Metropolitan Government observation decks, and the Shinjuku Gyoen garden.
- **Day trips**: Nikko, Kamakura, and Hakone (with Mount Fuji views) are accessible by train.

## Local Cuisine

- **Sushi and sashimi**: Options range from inexpensive conveyor-belt chains to high-end counters requiring reservations.
- **Ramen**: Regional styles are widely available; many shops use ticket vending machines for ordering.
- **Tempura, tonkatsu, and unagi**: Traditional specialties served in dedicated restaurants.
- **Izakaya**: Japanese pubs serving small dishes and drinks.
- **Convenience stores (konbini)**: 7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart sell high-quality, inexpensive prepared meals.
- **Depachika**: Department-store basement food halls with premium prepared foods.

Tipping is not practiced in Japan and may cause confusion. Many small restaurants remain cash-based, although card acceptance has grown substantially.

## Transportation

- **Airports**: Narita (NRT), about 60 km from the city, is connected by the Narita Express and Skyliner trains; Haneda (HND) is much closer, connected by monorail and the Keikyu line.
- **Trains and metro**: JR East lines (including the circular Yamanote Line), Tokyo Metro, and Toei Subway form a dense network. Services stop between roughly midnight and 05:00.
- **IC cards**: Suica and Pasmo cards (physical or in phone wallets) work on virtually all trains and buses and at many shops.
- **Japan Rail Pass**: Worthwhile only for extensive intercity Shinkansen travel, not for Tokyo alone.
- **Taxis**: Clean and reliable but expensive; rear doors open automatically. Ride-hailing apps mostly dispatch licensed taxis.
- **Walking**: Individual districts are best explored on foot; major station complexes are large, so extra time should be allowed for transfers.

## Budget Tips

- Budget travelers can manage on 8,000–12,000 JPY per day; mid-range travelers should plan 15,000–30,000 JPY per day including accommodation.
- Konbini meals, standing soba shops, and ramen keep food costs low without sacrificing quality.
- Free attractions include Meiji Shrine, Senso-ji, the Imperial Palace East Gardens, and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government observatory.
- Lunch set menus (teishoku) cost far less than dinner at the same restaurants.
- Business hotels and capsule hotels offer clean budget lodging.
- The rail network covers nearly everything, making taxis unnecessary for most visitors.

## Best Time to Visit

- **Late March–early April**: Cherry blossom season; scenic but the busiest and most expensive period.
- **April–May and October–November**: Mild, pleasant weather; November offers autumn foliage.
- **June–mid-July**: Rainy season with high humidity.
- **July–August**: Hot and humid (often 33–37°C), with typhoon risk extending into September.
- **Winter (December–February)**: Cold (2–12°C) but dry and sunny, with clear Mount Fuji views and fewer tourists.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Five to seven days is the ideal length for Tokyo itself, given the city's scale: this allows two to three days for the major districts (Shibuya, Shinjuku, Asakusa, Ueno, Akihabara), one day for museums or markets, and one or two day trips such as Kamakura or Hakone. Visitors combining Tokyo with Kyoto and Osaka typically plan a 10–14 day itinerary using the Shinkansen.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Obtain a Suica or Pasmo card immediately; it simplifies all transport and small purchases.
- Carry some cash; smaller restaurants and shrines are often cash-only.
- Eating while walking is generally avoided; consume food where it is purchased.
- Public trash cans are rare; carry rubbish until one is found.
- Stand on the left on Tokyo escalators, and queue in marked positions on train platforms.
- Speak quietly on trains; phone calls on board are considered rude.
- An eSIM or pocket Wi-Fi is advisable; free public Wi-Fi is inconsistent.
- Book popular attractions and restaurants (for example, the Ghibli Museum and Shibuya Sky) well in advance.
