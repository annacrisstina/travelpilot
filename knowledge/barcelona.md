# Barcelona

## Overview

Barcelona is the capital of Catalonia, an autonomous community in northeastern Spain, and the country's second-largest city with about 1.6 million residents. Located on the Mediterranean coast, the city is known for the modernist architecture of Antoni Gaudí, its medieval Gothic Quarter, urban beaches, and a strong food culture. Catalan and Spanish are both official languages. Barcelona is a dense, walkable city organized around distinct neighborhoods, including the medieval Ciutat Vella, the grid-planned Eixample, and the coastal Barceloneta.

## Top Attractions

- **Sagrada Família**: Gaudí's basilica, begun in 1882 and still under construction. Timed tickets should be booked online days or weeks in advance.
- **Park Güell**: A hillside park featuring Gaudí's mosaic work; the Monumental Zone requires a paid, timed ticket.
- **Casa Batlló and Casa Milà (La Pedrera)**: Gaudí-designed residential buildings on Passeig de Gràcia.
- **Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic)**: The medieval core, including Barcelona Cathedral and narrow historic streets.
- **La Rambla and La Boqueria Market**: The famous pedestrian boulevard and adjacent food market; busy and tourist-oriented.
- **Montjuïc**: A hill with museums, gardens, the 1992 Olympic facilities, and a castle accessible by cable car.
- **Picasso Museum**: A significant collection focused on the artist's early work, located in the El Born district.
- **Barceloneta Beach**: The city's main urban beach area.

## Local Cuisine

- **Tapas**: Small dishes such as patatas bravas, croquettes, and jamón ibérico.
- **Pa amb tomàquet**: Bread rubbed with tomato and olive oil, a Catalan staple served with most meals.
- **Seafood and rice dishes**: Paella and fideuà (a noodle-based variant) are common near the coast.
- **Crema catalana**: A custard dessert similar to crème brûlée.
- **Vermouth**: A pre-lunch vermouth ("fer el vermut") is a local weekend tradition.

Lunch is the main meal, typically eaten 14:00–16:00; dinner rarely starts before 21:00. The fixed-price lunch menu (menú del día) is the best value for a full restaurant meal.

## Transportation

- **Airport**: Barcelona–El Prat (BCN) is connected to the center by the Aerobús, metro line L9, and the R2 Nord commuter train.
- **Metro**: An extensive TMB network covering all major areas; service runs until midnight on weekdays, until 02:00 on Fridays, and all night on Saturdays.
- **Tickets**: The T-casual card (10 journeys) offers substantial savings over single tickets. The Hola Barcelona card provides unlimited travel for 2–5 days.
- **Buses and trams**: Complement the metro network for surface travel.
- **Walking and cycling**: The Eixample grid and old town are highly walkable; bike lanes are widespread.
- **Taxis**: Black-and-yellow official taxis are metered and reasonably priced; ride-hailing availability varies due to local regulation.

## Budget Tips

- Budget travelers can manage on 60–90 EUR per day; mid-range travelers should plan 120–200 EUR per day including accommodation.
- Book Sagrada Família and Park Güell online to avoid sold-out time slots.
- Many museums offer free entry on Sunday afternoons and the first Sunday of the month.
- Make lunch the main restaurant meal using menú del día offerings (typically 12–16 EUR).
- Avoid restaurants directly on La Rambla, where prices are high and quality inconsistent.
- City beaches and viewpoints such as the Bunkers del Carmel are free.

## Best Time to Visit

- **May–June and September–October** offer warm weather and manageable crowds; the sea is warm enough for swimming in late summer and early autumn.
- **July–August** is hot, humid, and extremely crowded, with peak accommodation prices.
- **November–March** is mild by European standards (10–16°C daytime), with fewer tourists and lower prices; some seaside businesses reduce their hours.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Three to four days is the ideal length for a first visit, covering the major Gaudí sites, the Gothic Quarter and El Born, Montjuïc, and time at the beach. Five days allows day trips to Montserrat, Sitges, or Girona.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Pickpocketing is the primary safety concern, particularly on La Rambla, the metro, and beaches. Keep phones and wallets secured.
- Many attractions enforce timed entry; last-minute tickets are often unavailable in high season.
- A tourist tax is charged per person per night at accommodations.
- Catalan is the primary local language; Spanish is universally understood, and English is common in tourist areas.
- Shops outside tourist zones often close for a midday break and on Sundays.
- Tap water is safe to drink, though some visitors prefer bottled water due to its mineral taste.
