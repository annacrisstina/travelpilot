# Travel Budgeting

## Budget Travel

Budget travel prioritizes low cost over comfort and convenience:

- Accommodation: hostels, guesthouses, budget hotels, or shared rentals.
- Food: street food, markets, supermarkets, and inexpensive local restaurants.
- Transport: public transportation, buses instead of trains or flights, and walking.
- Activities: free attractions, self-guided walking, parks, and free museum days.

Typical budget ranges: 40–70 EUR/USD per day in Western Europe and North America; 50–90 in expensive cities such as London, New York, Amsterdam, or Tokyo; 25–50 in Southern and Eastern Europe; and considerably less in much of Southeast Asia and Latin America.

## Mid-range Travel

Mid-range travel balances comfort and cost:

- Accommodation: three-star hotels, private rooms, or well-located apartments.
- Food: a mix of casual restaurants, one nicer meal per day, and occasional street food.
- Transport: public transit supplemented by occasional taxis or ride-hailing.
- Activities: paid attractions, guided tours, and pre-booked tickets.

Typical mid-range costs run 120–250 EUR/USD per day per person in major Western cities, with accommodation representing roughly half of the total.

## Luxury Travel

Luxury travel emphasizes comfort, service, and exclusivity:

- Accommodation: four- and five-star hotels or serviced apartments, often 300–1,000+ per night in major cities.
- Food: fine dining, tasting menus, and hotel restaurants.
- Transport: private transfers, business-class flights, and first-class rail.
- Activities: private guides, premium experiences, and priority access.

Luxury budgets typically begin around 400–500 EUR/USD per day per person and scale without practical limit.

## Typical Daily Costs

Approximate per-person daily costs in major Western European cities (mid-range unless noted):

- Hostel dorm bed: 25–50
- Three-star hotel double room: 100–200 (total, not per person)
- Quick lunch or street food: 8–15
- Sit-down dinner with drink: 25–50
- Public transport day pass: 5–10
- Major museum or attraction ticket: 15–30
- Coffee: 2–5

Costs in Tokyo and Seoul are comparable to Western Europe for food but often lower for transport; New York and Dubai skew higher for accommodation.

## Saving Money

- Travel in shoulder or low season, when flights and hotels can cost 30–50% less.
- Book flights early and be flexible on dates; midweek departures are usually cheaper.
- Make lunch the main restaurant meal; fixed-price lunch menus offer the same kitchens at lower prices.
- Use free museum days, city passes (when itineraries justify them), and free walking areas.
- Choose accommodation with kitchen access or breakfast included.
- Use public transport passes and fare-capping systems instead of taxis.
- Avoid dynamic currency conversion and airport currency exchange.
- Stay slightly outside the center in areas with fast transit connections.

## Common Travel Expenses

A complete trip budget should account for:

- **Pre-trip costs**: flights, insurance, visas, vaccinations, and gear.
- **Accommodation**: usually the largest single category; note city tourist taxes charged per night.
- **Food and drink**: typically 20–35% of daily spending.
- **Local transport**: airport transfers are often the single most expensive journey of the trip.
- **Attractions and activities**: entry fees, tours, and shows.
- **Connectivity**: eSIM or SIM costs, roaming charges.
- **Incidentals**: laundry, tips, luggage storage, and fees for card payments or ATM withdrawals.
- **Contingency**: a reserve of roughly 10–15% of the total budget for unplanned costs.

Tracking spending daily against a target, using a banking app or a simple note, is the most reliable way to stay within budget over a multi-week trip.
