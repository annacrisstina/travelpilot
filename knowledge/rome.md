# Rome

## Overview

Rome is the capital of Italy and one of the most historically significant cities in the world. Founded according to legend in 753 BC, the city served as the center of the Roman Empire and later became the seat of the Catholic Church. Today Rome has a population of approximately 2.8 million people and receives millions of visitors annually. The historic center is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and Vatican City, an independent city-state, lies entirely within Rome's boundaries. The city combines ancient ruins, Renaissance and Baroque architecture, and a modern urban environment.

## Top Attractions

- **Colosseum**: The largest ancient amphitheater ever built, completed in 80 AD. Advance booking is strongly recommended. Combined tickets typically include the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill.
- **Roman Forum and Palatine Hill**: The political, commercial, and religious center of ancient Rome, containing temple ruins, basilicas, and government buildings.
- **Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel**: One of the world's most important art collections, including Michelangelo's ceiling frescoes. Expect long queues without pre-booked tickets.
- **St. Peter's Basilica**: The largest church in the world, located in Vatican City. Entry is free, but a dress code applies (covered shoulders and knees).
- **Pantheon**: A remarkably preserved Roman temple from around 126 AD, now a church. A small entry fee applies.
- **Trevi Fountain**: An 18th-century Baroque fountain and one of the city's most visited landmarks.
- **Spanish Steps and Piazza Navona**: Notable public squares in the historic center.

## Local Cuisine

Roman cuisine is based on simple, traditional ingredients. Signature dishes include:

- **Carbonara**: Pasta with egg, pecorino cheese, guanciale, and black pepper.
- **Cacio e pepe**: Pasta with pecorino cheese and black pepper.
- **Amatriciana**: Pasta with tomato, guanciale, and pecorino.
- **Supplì**: Fried rice balls filled with mozzarella, a common street food.
- **Carciofi alla romana**: Roman-style braised artichokes, seasonal in winter and spring.

Trattorias in neighborhoods such as Trastevere and Testaccio generally offer more authentic food at lower prices than restaurants directly adjacent to major attractions. A standard Italian meal structure includes antipasto, primo (pasta), secondo (meat or fish), and dolce (dessert).

## Transportation

- **Airports**: Rome is served by Fiumicino (FCO), the main international airport, and Ciampino (CIA), used mainly by low-cost carriers. The Leonardo Express train connects Fiumicino to Termini station in about 32 minutes.
- **Metro**: Three lines (A, B, C) cover major areas, though coverage of the historic center is limited due to archaeological constraints.
- **Buses and trams**: An extensive network operated by ATAC. Tickets must be validated on board.
- **Tickets**: A single BIT ticket is valid for 100 minutes across metro, bus, and tram. Daily and multi-day passes are available.
- **Taxis**: Use official white taxis with meters. Fixed fares apply between the airports and the city center.
- **Walking**: The historic center is compact and best explored on foot.

## Budget Tips

- Budget travelers can manage on approximately 60–90 EUR per day; mid-range travelers should expect 120–200 EUR per day including accommodation.
- Coffee consumed standing at a bar is cheaper than table service.
- Many churches, including St. Peter's Basilica, are free to enter.
- The Roma Pass offers public transport and discounted museum entry for 48 or 72 hours.
- Drinking water is free from public fountains (nasoni) throughout the city.
- State museums offer free admission on certain days, typically the first Sunday of each month.

## Best Time to Visit

- **Spring (April–June)** and **autumn (September–October)** offer mild weather and are the most comfortable periods.
- **Summer (July–August)** is hot, often exceeding 35°C, and crowded; many local businesses close in mid-August.
- **Winter (November–February)** is the low season, with cooler temperatures around 8–14°C, fewer crowds, and lower prices.

## Suggested Trip Duration

Three to four days is the ideal length for a first visit. A typical allocation is one day for ancient Rome (Colosseum, Forum, Palatine Hill), one day for the Vatican, and one to two days for the historic center, the Pantheon area, and neighborhoods such as Trastevere. Visitors planning day trips to Ostia Antica or Tivoli should add an extra day.

## Practical Travel Tips

- Book Colosseum and Vatican tickets online well in advance.
- Validate public transport tickets to avoid fines.
- Beware of pickpockets on crowded buses (notably line 64) and around major attractions.
- Restaurants typically serve dinner from 19:30 onward; earlier service is generally aimed at tourists.
- A tourist tax of a few euros per person per night is charged by accommodations.
- Tap water is safe to drink.
- Modest dress (covered shoulders and knees) is required for entry to churches.
